There is nothing exciting about trade show logistics, but they are what makes or breaks your first hours on site. A queue avoided, a route planned ahead, a well-placed hotel: all of it gives back minutes to what actually matters. This guide brings together the essentials so logistics is never the problem.

## The Badge: Always Collect It Online

Most trade shows are free for visitors but require prior registration. Do it online before you arrive: you will receive a badge to print or show on your phone, and you will avoid the registration queue on site, which can be long when the doors open on day one. Registering ahead of time also often unlocks useful services: booking meetings with exhibitors, a personalised programme, access to certain conferences by reservation.

## Access: Plan for the Last Mile

Large exhibition centres are generally well served, but the last mile can hold a few surprises. Check in advance which public transport serves the site, how frequent it is, and the real travel time from your starting point. If you are driving, locate the car park and its rate, often steep at major venues, and plan for traffic at peak times. Some shows offer shuttles from train stations or airports: a small detail that changes your whole day.

## The Right Hall, the Right Day

At a show spread across several halls, knowing where to head first avoids exhausting back-and-forth trips. Identify the halls that match your objectives before you arrive. Also think about which day to choose: the first day is often the busiest for both attendance and exhibitor energy, the last can be quieter and better suited to in-depth conversations, though some stands start winding down. If you have key meetings, the middle of the show is often the best compromise.

## Accommodation: Proximity Versus Budget

For a show lasting two days or more, or if you are travelling from far away, staying overnight is generally better than a daily round trip that eats into your time and energy. The right trade-off is between proximity and budget: hotels closest to the exhibition centre are convenient but fill up fast and see their rates climb during major shows. Accommodation a little further out but well connected by transport often offers better value. Book early: during a major show, nearby availability disappears quickly.

## On Site: Practical Habits

A few details make the difference over two days on your feet. Comfortable shoes, a way to charge your phone, a water bottle since you hydrate little and talk a lot. Plan how you will exchange contact details efficiently and where you will store the documentation you collect, or better, ask for digital versions so you are not weighed down. Spot the catering and rest areas on arrival: at large shows, they fill up fast at peak times.

## In Summary

A successful trade show visit starts with logistics settled in advance: badge collected online, access and transport checked, priority halls identified, accommodation booked early if needed, and a few practical habits to go the distance.
