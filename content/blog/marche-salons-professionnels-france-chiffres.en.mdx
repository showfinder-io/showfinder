How many trade shows are held in France each year, who attends them, and what do they weigh in the economy? The data exists, but it stays scattered across institutional reports that no one translates into plain language. Here is the sourced, data-driven overview of the market, drawn from the reference public sources, updated with every new release.

## The Number to Remember: A Sector Back in Balance

The Event Data Book 2026, published by UNIMEV in July 2026 and covering 2025 data, marks a turning point. For the first time since 2019, every indicator for the sector has turned positive. The French events industry now weighs 10.4 billion euros in revenue, carried by close to 4,000 companies and close to 38,000 employees. After several years of restructuring, the events sector has returned to its pre-health-crisis level, and now claims a status as a pillar of national economic attractiveness rather than an auxiliary sector.

## A Growing Sector, Driven by B2B

After the years of disruption caused by the pandemic, the French trade show market has regained a growth dynamic. Over 2025, the UNIMEV Observatoire reports growth across every indicator: number of exhibitors, visits and net floor space sold are all trending upward. The engine behind this recovery is clearly the professional segment. According to the UNIMEV Observatoire, B2B trade shows recorded a rise in attendance of more than 12% in 2025 and a growth of close to 5% in the number of exhibitors year on year, figures clearly higher than those of consumer events. Shows with an international dimension show the strongest gains, a sign that France's appeal has been restored.

## Attendance and Exhibitors: The Orders of Magnitude

The Event Data Book gives the industry's reference measure, based on certified data from the OJS-UNIMEV database. The 2025 edition, covering 2024, analysed a sample of 448 shows audited by the certification bodies Expostat and Expocert, including 193 B2B trade shows, supplemented by a focus on 114 international shows. Over that exercise, visits and the number of exhibitors grew by around 3% compared with the previous year.

Two structural findings emerge from these successive editions. First, foreign exhibitors again account for around 41% of exhibitors, confirming the international dimension of the French market. Second, exhibition floor space has stayed durably below its 2019 level (around 89%), reflecting an underlying shift towards more compact, leaner formats: stands are getting smaller, but attendance is picking back up.

## Paris as the Locomotive, the Regions in Support

Île-de-France retains its role as the historic epicentre of the market, driven by the major Paris exhibition centres. But growth is not confined to the Paris region: according to the UNIMEV Observatoire, events held in the regions fully contribute to the momentum, with some years showing exhibitor growth above the national average. The market therefore runs on two legs, which widens the choice for visitors and exhibitors alike.

## Three Underlying Trends

Leaner formats. The sustained drop in floor space compared with 2019 reflects a rationalisation: more compact stands, more focused events, growing attention to environmental footprint, which has become a major selection criterion for organisers and participants alike.

More qualified attendance. The sector is emphasising a more targeted audience over a race for volume: a show's value is increasingly measured by the quality of the encounters it enables, not just the number of entries.

Confirmed international appeal. With a stable share of foreign exhibitors and international shows leading the growth, France retains its rank among the world's major hubs for professional events.

## Why These Figures Matter to You

For a visitor, this data says that a trade show remains a live, growing channel, provided you choose it well. For an exhibitor, it confirms that in-person B2B is regaining its effectiveness, while still favouring measured stand formats over floor space at any cost.
