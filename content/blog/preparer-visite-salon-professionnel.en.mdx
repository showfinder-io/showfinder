You have chosen the right show. Now you just need to avoid wasting the two days you will spend there. The difference between a visitor who leaves with three qualified contacts and a project moving forward, and one who leaves exhausted with a stack of brochures, almost never comes down to the show itself: it comes down to preparation. Here is how to approach your visit like a business meeting, not an outing.

## Three to Four Weeks Before: Set Your Objectives and Book Meetings

Start by writing down, in plain terms, what you are coming to achieve: source suppliers for a specific need, compare competing solutions, attend a regulatory conference, meet a partner. Two or three clear objectives are enough; beyond that, you will spread yourself too thin. These objectives shape everything else in your preparation. Use this lead time to request meetings: most exhibitors you want to see are far more available if you lock in a slot in advance rather than hoping to catch them between other visitors. A simple message explaining who you are and what you are looking for is often enough to secure a quality conversation.

## One to Two Weeks Before: Study the Floor Plan and Programme

Get hold of the exhibitor list and the show floor plan, almost always available online. Identify the stands that match your objectives and note their location: at a large show spread across several halls, time lost finding your way can add up to hours. Do the same with the conference programme: pick out in advance the two or three sessions genuinely worth attending, and block them in your diary as firm appointments. The best conferences fill up fast, so it is better to arrive early.

## The Day Before: Logistics That Save Time

Collect your badge online to avoid the queue at the entrance, which can be long on the morning of day one. Check access to the venue: public transport, parking, shuttles. Prepare an efficient way to exchange contact details, and charge your devices. If the show runs two days and you are travelling from far away, staying overnight is often better than a daily round trip that eats into your useful time and energy.

## Show Day: Follow Your Plan, Keep Some Margin

Start with your firm meetings and chosen conferences: these are your priorities, everything else is a bonus. In between, walk the aisles you identified beforehand, but keep a little free time for unplanned discoveries, which sometimes make the real value of a show. Take notes immediately after every important conversation: a name, a need, a next step. By the end of two days, you will not remember who was who if you have not written it down.

## After the Show: Follow Up Within Two Weeks

A show only pays off if you follow up. In the days that follow, reconnect with the people you met, recalling the context of your conversation: this is what turns a business card into a real opportunity. After two weeks, the memory effect fades and the follow-up loses its impact. Sort your notes, identify concrete next steps, and schedule them. This discipline, more than the show itself, determines your return on investment.

## In Summary

Preparing your visit means spreading the effort over time: objectives and meetings three weeks out, studying the plan and programme in the final fortnight, logistics sorted the day before, disciplined execution on the day, and follow-up within two weeks. None of these steps is complicated; it is how they chain together that makes the difference between two wasted days and two profitable ones.
