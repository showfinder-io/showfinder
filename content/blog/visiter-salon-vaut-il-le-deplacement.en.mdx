The question is a fair one. At a time when negotiations happen over video calls, suppliers are compared online, and every exhibitor has a website, why devote two days and a trip to a trade show? At Agoris, our answer is neither a reflexive yes nor a defensive no: it depends. Here are the cases where the trip is fully justified, and those where it is not.

## What Digital Has Genuinely Replaced

Let us be honest: part of what people used to go to shows for is now available online. Comparing spec sheets, getting a first quote, watching a product demo, staying up to date with a sector's news: all of this can now be done from your desk. For these uses alone, travelling adds little. If your only objective is to grab a brochure or a price list, a call or a website will do.

## What In-Person Attendance Still Delivers

But a show has never served only that purpose. Its real value lies in what resists remote substitutes. Handling a product, physically comparing two solutions side by side, sizing up a counterpart face to face before building a business relationship: none of this transfers properly to a screen. A show also condenses into two days a density of encounters, including unplanned ones, that no online prospecting reproduces. You pick up on the mood of a market, you run into a competitor, you discover a player you were not looking for. This serendipity is precisely what digital, organised around what you already know you want, cannot offer.

## When the Trip Is Fully Justified

Travel when your objective calls for real contact: sourcing a strategic supplier you want to meet before committing, comparing equipment you need to see and touch, building or maintaining relationships in a sector where trust is built in person, attending conferences whose calibre and speakers justify the visit on their own, or taking the pulse of a fast-changing market. In these situations, no digital channel replaces being there.

## When It Is Not Necessary

Conversely, stay home if you are only coming to collect brochures, if no genuinely relevant exhibitor is present, if the show is declining and you have no specific objective, or if the cost of the trip, team time included, clearly exceeds the expected benefit. Going to a show out of habit, because you go every year, is the worst possible reason.

## The Right Question Is Not Digital vs In-Person

Pitting the two against each other is a false debate. The most effective visitors combine both: they prepare online, travel for what requires it, and follow up online after the show. Digital has not killed the trade show, it has raised its bar. A show deserves the trip when it gives you what a screen cannot. The rest of the time, your computer is enough.
